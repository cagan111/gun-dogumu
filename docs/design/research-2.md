## 1. SQUIRE (getsquire.com)
- **Positioning/tone:** "All-in-one barbershop management platform"; POS, payments, payroll, multi-location. Tone is masculine, operator-first, and talks about "elevating" barbers. [S] [company](https://getsquire.com/company), [business-edge](https://www.getsquire.com/business-edge/barbershop-software), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1380764)
- **Hero:** [K] Dark, high-contrast hero with a bold headline, a CTA pair (demo / app download) and barber photography.
- **Product display:** [K] Phone mockups for the consumer app, dashboard screenshots for owners, and a split between barber and client audiences. App Store listing: [apps.apple.com](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1254375220).
- **Type/color:** [K] Heavy sans headlines on black/charcoal with one bright accent. Reads "streetwear / barbershop".
- **Imagery:** [K] Real barbers, fades and chairs, high contrast.
- **Booking flow:** [K] Pick shop, then barber, then service, then slot. The barber is the unit, with profile photos and a favorites mechanic.
- **Dashboard:** [K] Per-barber columns, with payroll and commission reporting as a selling point. Content marketing pushes reporting ("record vs. reporting") [S].
- **Trust:** [S] Y Combinator, since 2015, "State of Barbershops" report.
- **Premium vs generic:** Premium in its culture fluency; generic in the SaaS feature-grid sections underneath.
- **Brilliant:** Speaks barber culture and treats the barber as a brand.
- **Weakness:** Black-and-chrome masculinity excludes women's salons and feels corporate-hip.

## 2. Vagaro
- **Positioning/tone:** Broad marketplace and all-in-one for every beauty/wellness category. Friendly, utilitarian, price-led. [K]
- **Hero:** [K] Headline, signup form or CTA, a category selector, and a screenshot collage.
- **Product display:** [K] Many screenshots of calendar, marketplace and app, with little motion.
- **Type/color:** [K] Neutral sans, blue/teal brand color, standard SaaS.
- **Imagery:** [K] Stock-style smiling professionals.
- **Booking flow:** Easy and payment is on the same screen [S]. But clients are often forced to create a Vagaro account, which confuses them [S]. [G2](https://g2.com/products/vagaro/reviews?page=2), [GetApp](https://www.getapp.com/retail-consumer-services-software/a/vagaro/reviews/)

- **Premium vs generic:** Generic. Function over feeling.
- **Brilliant:** Breadth, with one price covering everything.
- **Weakness:** Dense UI, forced accounts, and the client-facing surface looks like Vagaro rather than the salon.

## 3. Boulevard (joinblvd.com)
- **Positioning/tone:** High-end salons/spas, "client experience" and brand fit. Editorial, confident, fashion-adjacent. Customer stories lead the marketing. [S] [Rachel Paige](https://www.joinblvd.com/customer-stories/case-study-rachel-paige), [Spoke & Weal](https://www.joinblvd.com/blog/spoke-and-weal)
- **Hero:** [K] Large editorial photograph or video, short serif or refined sans headline, and a single CTA.
- **Product display:** [K] Restrained UI crops inside photographed scenes, not screenshot walls.
- **Type:** Rebranded in April 2023 by [Defining](https://defining.com/projects/boulevard/). The art director said they chose open-source typefaces on purpose, clean and easy for partners to find. [S] [design Q&A](https://www.joinblvd.com/blog/boulevard-design-wood-simmons-qa)
- **Color/imagery:** [K] Soft neutrals, mostly monochrome, with beauty photography, hair detail and strong crops. Case-study image alt text such as "yellow comb pulling through glossy dark brown hair" confirms macro hair imagery [S].
- **Booking flow:** The pitch is a booking experience that matches the salon's own brand, replacing an "off-brand and cumbersome" one. Reported: -80% no-shows, +23% prebooked [S] (vendor case study, weigh lightly). A new client "was blown away by the design" [S].
- **Dashboard:** [K] Calm, light calendar with pastel appointment blocks and a clean client sidebar.
- **Trust:** Named salon logos and case-study numbers, so social proof is the product.
- **Premium vs generic:** The most premium of the five. Space, photography and few words.
- **Brilliant:** Sells the clients' brand, not its own.
- **Weakness:** Enterprise-gated (demo, pricing hidden). Too refined for a neighborhood barber.

- **Type/color:** Templates, color palettes and three "designer-approved" themes, with an accent color chosen at setup [S] [blog](https://glossgenius.com/blog/how-to-build-a-booking-website). Brand site: [K] soft serif or rounded display plus clean sans, blush/cream/black.
- **Imagery:** [K] Lifestyle and portfolio-style beauty shots; reviewers praise "premium beauty-inspired design" [S].

## Cross-product

### Clichés to avoid
1. Hero = headline, two CTAs, and a floating laptop plus phone mockup pair.
2. Pastel gradient blobs, glassmorphism cards, purple/indigo SaaS accent.
3. Stock "smiling stylist with tablet" photography.
4. Logo wall, then three-icon feature grid, then star-rating strip.
5. Dark mode "barber" aesthetic: black, gold, crossed razors, scissors-and-comb icons.
6. Dashboard screenshot as the only proof of product.
7. Generic booking stepper: service, staff, time, details, with a rote progress bar.
8. Fake "booked 3 min ago" popups and countdown urgency.

### Gaps a Cyprus product can own
- **Place as the brand.** None of the five feels located. Use Mediterranean light: limewash, terracotta, olive, sea-blue ink, the sand-colored stone of the Kyrenia coast, and carob/lemon accents. Warm paper off-whites instead of cold white.
- **Crafted, tactile type.** Pair a characterful serif or slab display face (Latin plus Turkish ğ ı İ ş ç ö ü must render beautifully) with a quiet sans. Set bilingual TR/EN pairs as a design feature, not a toggle afterthought.
- **Real people.** Photograph actual north Cyprus barbers and salons (Girne, Lefkoşa, Gazimağusa): hands, razors, tea glasses, shutters, tile floors. Documentary, grainy-warm, not stock. This is Boulevard's craft level at neighborhood scale.
- **Barber culture without the swagger.** The Turkish and Cypriot berber ritual (hot towel, çay or kahve, conversation, regulars) is a richer language than black-and-chrome. Make the "usual" (Her zamanki gibi) a first-class booking shortcut.
- **Local rhythms.** Bayram and holiday hours, Friday-heavy weeks, siesta-style closing, WhatsApp as the default confirmation channel, TL/£/€ pricing awareness, phone-number-first identity with no forced account (beating Vagaro's friction).
- **The salon's page, but warmer.** GlossGenius shows the value of a branded client page. Offer fewer, handcrafted themes tied to local aesthetics, with a real portfolio and embeddable widget (beating GlossGenius's walled garden).
- **Calendar with personality.** Appointment blocks as tactile "cards" in a warm palette, not blue/grey grids; per-chair columns that feel like a shop floor; low density and big touch targets (Vagaro's "labyrinth" is the anti-model).
- **Motion, small and meaningful.** Slot confirmation as a stamped or receipt-like flourish, a hot-towel-steam style soft fade, no confetti. Respect reduced-motion.
- **Trust, local style.** Named local shops and owner quotes in Turkish and English, a visible "made in KKTC" note, WhatsApp support, and honest pricing instead of logo walls and star strips.

### 10-line summary (for the caller)
1. Fetch was blocked for all five domains; findings mix search snippets [S] and prior knowledge [K]. Re-verify visuals later.
2. Boulevard is the premium benchmark: editorial photography, restrained UI, open-source type, and it sells the salon's brand, not its own.
3. GlossGenius's strongest idea is the branded client booking page, but it is a walled garden (no widget embed, US-only).
4. Vagaro's lessons in what to avoid: forced client accounts, nav-tab "labyrinth", generic stock feel.
5. Square wins on zero-friction onboarding and trust-through-plainness, but the client experience is anonymous and barely customizable.
6. SQUIRE nails barber-culture fluency but locks into a black, chrome masculine look that excludes women's salons.
7. Shared clichés: device-mockup hero, pastel/purple SaaS gradients, stock smiling pros, logo wall plus icon grid, fake urgency.
8. Open lane: a located, Mediterranean, warm, tactile identity with real local photography; none of the five feels like a place.
9. Product wedges: no-account phone/WhatsApp-first booking, "the usual" rebook, bilingual TR/EN as design, festival/holiday hours.
10. Calendar: warm, low-density, big-target, per-chair columns; small meaningful motion; local named social proof.
